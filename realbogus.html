Real or bogus?

When GECKO follows up a gravitational-wave alert with KMTNet, the pipeline subtracts a reference image from each new image and flags what is left. Most of it is not a real source but poor subtractions, residuals of bright stars, cosmic rays and detector defects. A machine-learning classifier gives each candidate a score, and the team then checks them by eye. Try it: look at the new, reference and difference images and decide. The candidate is in the small box at the centre.

NewReferenceDifference

Keys: R for real, B for bogus, N or space for the next one.

18 candidates (6 real, 12 bogus) from the GECKO KMTNet follow-up of S240422ed and S250206dm, labelled by the team. Real ones are sources already known before the alert, such as variable stars and supernovae; none is a counterpart of the gravitational-wave event. The order is random on each visit.
