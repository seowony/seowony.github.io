Research Associate Professor

Seo-Won Chang

Research Institute of Basic Sciences, Seoul National University

I am an observational astronomer working in time-domain and multi-messenger astronomy. To study how the sky changes on timescales from minutes to years, I have spent the past decade building end-to-end infrastructure for wide-field surveys — high-precision photometric pipelines, spatio-temporal databases, and real-time transient-detection systems — for facilities including SkyMapper, KMTNet, and the 7-Dimensional Telescope (7DT).

As first-author lead of the KS4 Data Release 1 (Chang et al. 2026, JKAS), I delivered KMTNet's first public survey release to the community through NOIRLab Astro Data Lab and CDS. In 2017 I participated in the GW170817/AT2017gfo discovery — the first optical counterpart of a gravitational-wave event. I am also a Visiting Fellow at the Research School of Astronomy & Astrophysics, Australian National University.

Research

Four connected lines

Stellar magnetic activity: flares, starspots, and the activity–age relation

Magnetically active M dwarfs — their flares and starspot-induced rotational variability — and the age–rotation–activity relation, established through large-scale photometric measurements from SkyMapper including the M-dwarf flaring fraction and a spatially dependent activity–kinematic-age relation across the solar cylinder. Now extending to FGK dwarfs and toward spectroscopic detection of coronal mass ejections on Sun-like host stars.

Blue Large-Amplitude Pulsators and the puzzle of the missing envelope

BLAPs are a recently recognized class of hot pulsating stars — nearly 200 found toward the Galactic bulge since their 2017 discovery by OGLE. Their evolution requires the loss of most of the stellar envelope, yet unlike hot subdwarfs (sdBs), BLAPs show few binary companions; the formation channel remains actively debated. I discovered a new BLAP in SkyMapper and characterized it as a helium-deficient, thick-disk star — a candidate for an alternative formation channel (Chang et al. 2024, MNRAS).

Time-domain survey infrastructure and AI-based classification

End-to-end infrastructure for time-domain discovery: high-precision photometric pipelines, spatio-temporal databases, and the public release of wide-field surveys — most recently as first-author lead of the KS4 Data Release 1. On the methodological side, machine-learning classification of variable-star types, periodic variables, and real/bogus transients, with the goal of decision-aware AI for LSST-scale time-domain astronomy.

Optical counterparts of gravitational-wave events

From early 2017 through the O4 run, the optical search for gravitational-wave counterparts: participating in the GW170817/AT2017gfo discovery, building and operating the SkyMapper alert follow-up pipeline through LIGO/Virgo O3 (Chang et al. 2021, PASA), and continuing follow-up with KMTNet and 7DT within GECKO. Now expanding to the science of the sources themselves — kilonovae from NS–NS and NS–BH mergers, and single-epoch SED-based identification of orphan kilonovae in the Rubin/LSST alert stream.

On sky now

Programmes I lead — 7DT, 7DS, BOES, Gemini

Photometric timing of BLAP candidates beyond the Galactic bulge

A search for ultraviolet variability in SkyMapper DR4 has identified more than one hundred BLAP candidates in southern fields, outside the crowded Galactic bulge where nearly all known BLAPs have been found. Multiple 7DT units observe each target simultaneously in g and r; their interleaved exposures produce continuous light curves that constrain periods to ~0.1% precision down to 19th magnitude within two to three nights of half-hour monitoring blocks.

With Christian Wolf, Christopher Onken, Michael Bessell (ANU) · Péter Németh (Czech Academy of Sciences) · 7DT core members

Chromospheric Hα activity of southern K and M dwarfs from 7DS

Hα emission is the clearest chromospheric indicator of magnetic activity in late-type stars, but existing large samples are predominantly northern and spectroscopic. The 7DS medium-band spectrophotometry (400–900 nm) provides Hα-excess measurements across the entire survey footprint. With multi-epoch coverage, quiescent chromospheric activity can be separated from flare events on timescales from days to months.

With 7DT core members · rotation periods from TESS and K2, ages from Gaia kinematics

Minute-cadence spectrophotometric evolution of stellar flares

Stellar flares are commonly modelled as a single ~10,000 K blackbody, but the most energetic events show complex temperature structure during the decay: the blue continuum remains near 8,000–11,000 K while the red end cools to 3,500–5,500 K. Rubin/LSST will detect such events by the thousand, but in only two filters per visit. This programme triggers 7DT follow-up within minutes of an alert, tracking the full 400–900 nm spectral shape — including Hα — through the entire flare decay.

With 7DT core members

High-resolution spectroscopy of chromospheric line evolution in stellar flares

While photometry records flare energetics, time-resolved high-resolution spectroscopy reveals the physical conditions of the flaring chromosphere. Using the Bohyunsan Optical Echelle Spectrograph (BOES), we obtain cadenced spectra through individual energetic flares — tracking the evolution of Hα, the Balmer series, and other chromospheric lines minute by minute as the continuum evolves independently. These line profiles provide the diagnostic window for detecting filament eruptions and associated coronal mass ejections.

Latest Chromospheric line evolution during an energetic flare on AD Leo — Oh et al., JKAS (corresponding author)

BLAP formation channels: Gemini spectroscopy and non-LTE model atmospheres

Pulsation periods alone cannot discriminate among formation channels — binary mass transfer, mergers, and supernova-survivor scenarios each predict distinct surface temperatures, gravities, and abundance patterns. Only ~15% of the roughly 200 known BLAPs have measured atmospheric parameters. This NRF Core Research programme (2026–2029) uses Gemini spectroscopy with non-LTE model-atmosphere analysis (XTgrid / TLUSTY) to build the first homogeneous spectroscopic characterization of the BLAP class.

With Péter Németh (Czech Academy of Sciences & Astroserver.org) — XTgrid / TLUSTY modelling · Gemini programme G-2025B-0498-V

Rubin / LSST

Where my work meets the survey

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time will generate millions of transient alerts each night. My research topics — stellar flares, BLAPs, kilonovae — all require rapid identification within this alert stream followed by prompt multi-wavelength follow-up.

I am building the path from the Rubin alert stream to minute-cadence medium-band follow-up with 7DT: cross-matching alerts against K–M dwarf catalogues, deriving per-event colour temperatures, and setting trigger criteria for events that merit immediate spectrophotometric follow-up. The same framework applies to single-epoch SED-based identification of orphan kilonovae without gravitational-wave triggers (Paek et al. 2026, ApJ).

In parallel, I am pursuing time-domain studies combining 7DS medium-band spectrophotometry with SPHEREx infrared spectral surveys to classify and characterize variable and transient sources across the optical–infrared range. This builds on a decade of survey infrastructure development: the SkyMapper alert follow-up pipeline through LIGO/Virgo O3, the KS4 public data release, and the 7DT data-management portal.
