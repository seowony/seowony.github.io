Research / GW170817, 17–18 August 2017

Why I rang the bell while everyone was in bed

It was the night of our monthly LIGO–Virgo EM follow-up telecon, so I was at my desk from about 11 pm, an hour before the meeting.

At 11:08 pm a gravitational-wave alert landed in my inbox, and the chance that it involved a neutron star was 100%. I wasn't rattled, because I'd been in a similar situation a few months earlier. I checked whether our SkyMapper follow-up pipeline was working properly. It wasn't. The database hadn't updated, so I entered the event by hand and switched on target-of-opportunity mode.

About 30 minutes later things moved fast. Fermi and INTEGRAL reported high-energy signals from the same part of the sky. At 11:51 pm Anais and I got an email from Jeff Cooke with the subject line "Urgent".

Hi Anais and Seo-Won,

There is a coincident detection between LIGO Hanford and Fermi. The localisation from Fermi is something like a square degree.

Can you get SkyMapper on it immediately, if you aren't doing so already?

Thanks!
Jeff

They wanted to stop our blind survey, by pressing the telescope's emergency button if need be, and point straight at the Fermi area, which was now only a few square degrees. As far as I knew, only Chris Onken, Chris Wolf and Bill could take over the telescope like that. But it was late on a Thursday night, so emails and phone calls got nowhere, and Chris Onken was away on a family holiday with no way to reach him. Jeff and Igor suggested I contact the staff at Siding Spring Observatory. I spoke to a staff member, but it didn't get us much further.

Jeff kept asking where I was, and then suggested I simply go to Chris Wolf's house. OMG.

So at 1:45 am I drove to Chris's house and, very carefully, pressed the doorbell. Nobody expects someone at the door at that hour, so Chris and his family were understandably cautious about the stranger outside. Luckily, even in the dark, we recognised each other. :)

This was just the beginning of the story.

And the questions for the next run:

  • Hit the southern sky fields! We now know how to fix the problems, and our team will be on the telescope.
  • With a little luck, will we discover a new kilonova?
  • Or will we face a new kind of complication?
  • …
